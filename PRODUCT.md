# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Tennis players first: anyone who plays matches and wants to get better, from a weekend league player to a college or aspiring-pro competitor, plus the coaches and programs who work with them. Most have never used tennis analytics and do not know its vocabulary. Many arrive cold, from a creator's video or a shared link, on a phone, with no idea what Advantage is. The page has about three seconds to tell them, in words any player understands: film your match, upload it, see how to win the next one. They self-serve and sign up for a free account; coaches sign up the same way and find their team inside the app.

## Product Purpose

Advantage turns a video of your match into an explanation of it. A player films from behind the baseline on a phone, uploads the file, and gets back where they won points, where they lost them, and what to work on, each linked to the moment on video. Underneath that sits the serious product (multi-match trends, opponent scouting, AI-surfaced insights, coach-ready reports), and it is still there for the player or coach who wants depth. But the landing page leads with the outcome, not the machinery. Analysis like this used to belong to tour-level analysts; Advantage hands it to anyone with a phone and a fence to clip it to. Success is a free signup from someone who had never heard of us a minute ago.

## Brand Personality

Human, clear, encouraging. The voice is a good coach talking to a player after a match: warm, direct, specific, never showing off. Three words: **friendly, plain-spoken, credible**. The emotional goal on landing is *"oh, I could do that"*: the visitor sees people like them, understands the promise without translating it, and trusts that there is real substance behind it once they scroll.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS template.** No hero-metric cliche, no identical icon-heading-text card grids, no gradient-blob backgrounds, no stock illustration, no stock-photo smiles. If it could be any B2B tool, it has failed.
- **Stat-forward and insider.** A wall of numbers and analytics vocabulary as the first impression. It reads as "not for me" to most players.
- **Overdesigned / loud.** No excessive animation, no glassmorphism-everywhere, no competing accent colors, no noise textures for their own sake. Restraint signals premium.
- Specifically avoid v0's three inconsistent blues, decorative glowing orbs, and typing-text gimmicks.

## Design Principles

1. **People first, product second.** The hero is a real photograph of real players: setting up a phone, laughing at the net, mid-rally. The dashboard appears beside them as a smaller supporting card ("the person and their stats"), never as the hero itself. No stock photography that could belong to any brand; shoot our own (`assets/PHOTO_SHOTLIST.md`).
2. **Plain language over jargon.** Say what the player gets in words they already use: "where you won points, where you lost them, what to work on." No ELC, no index names, no "aggregated trends" above the fold. Term-of-art vocabulary is allowed further down the page, next to the screen that explains it. Still banned: empty hype like "take your game to the next level."
3. **Show it, then prove it.** Lead with the outcome and the three steps; follow with real product UI as the proof. Every screenshot is the actual dashboard, never an abstract graphic, and never carries invented stats, testimonials or user counts.
4. **Understood in three seconds, with zero context.** A visitor from a creator's link must be able to say what Advantage does after one glance at the hero, and must see the sign-up button without scrolling on a phone.
5. **Earned motion.** Animation only when it reveals or clarifies on scroll. Never decorative. Always honors reduced-motion.
6. **One blue.** A single committed accent (`#3B82F6`) carries every highlight, CTA, and focus state, exactly as the product does. Neutrals are tinted, never competing.
7. **Stand on our own.** Never name or compare against another product anywhere on the page.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: AA contrast on all text and CTAs, full keyboard navigation with visible focus rings, semantic HTML, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` path that disables scroll reveals and animation. Extra care on motion since the page uses scroll-triggered reveals.
